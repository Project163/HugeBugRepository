Title

MoveTest fails intermittently

for i in `seq 1 50`; do runTest MoveTest >> moveTestFailures.txt;done

 Edit: had this as Windows-only - turns out it's failing on on *nix as well
Discussion
- PendingRangeCalculatorService.instance.blockUntilFinished() doesn't actually block until all operations are complete as we can have a race between the pendingRangeTask and of whomever was blocking on the prior function.  A simple 100ms sleep on the unit test resolves the problem, however we have 3 places in StorageService that are relying on completion of blockUntilFinished() that now seem like prime targets for this race. 

 I'm thinking a refcount between update() and PendingRangeTask() w/an atomic inside PendingRangeCalculatorService to have blockUntilFinished() actually do what it claims to do should resolve this but I'm not too keen on adding that in there unless absolutely necessary.
- Just noticed - the logic inside blockUntilFinished  does  check for pending jobs, so my previous comment about the function racing w/pendingRangeTasks is incorrect.  That being said, there definitely is still a race in play here.
- ... [Middle Discussions Snipped for Brevity] ...
- The v3 patch still has a bit of a race condition: 

  
     if  (updateJobs.get() < 2)
   {
       updateJobs.incrementAndGet();
       executor.submit( new  PendingRangeTask());
   }
 
  

 The increment and check need to happen atomically instead of in two separate calls.  For example, you could do: 

  
     if  (updateJobs.incrementAndGet() <= 2)
       executor.submit( new  PendingRangeTask());
    else 
       updateJobs.decrementAndGet();
 
  

 Either that, or you could always increment and submit a task regardless of the current count and use the rejected execution handler to decrement the count.
- Good catch on the race - totally slipped by me.  I like the symmetry of "whatever the thread pool does will take care of decrementing" rather than branching on the update() call.  Attaching a v4 w/the race addressed and a new RejectedExecutionHandler.
