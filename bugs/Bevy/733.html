- I won't pretend to understand the details of startup and scheduling, but I do want to talk a bit about initialization in actual games that am familiar with. Take for example something like Witcher 3: there are a lot of different modes that happen before actual "gameplay" begins: title screens, splash screens, intro movie, main menu, and so on. And given that most of these modes are relatively lightweight in terms of graphic resources, I wouldn't assume that all of the shaders and assets are being held in a "ready" state while in these modes. This means that a transition from the main menu to the gameplay mode involves a lot of loading and initialization, including a progress bar which animates during the transition. Only when everything is ready do we actually present a 3d view of the game world.

This makes me think that the kinds of things that you might want to do in RenderStartup are things you might also want to do in major mode transitions generally. Of course, there are some initializations that only make sense at the very start of the app. But "start" isn't as much of a special case as people might think.
- I don't think I agree. When transitioning from a main menu to a gameplay mode, it's generally ok to keep rendering the previous frame, since almost definitely those assets will remain loaded until the new scene is ready. Or in other words, it's fine if there's some "latency" between wanting to transition and the new assets being ready, since the loading screen is ready to be rendered.

In contrast, startup doesn't have this "safety net". There's nothing that's "safe" to render, since nothing may be loaded yet! Even the text of the main menu may still be loading.
